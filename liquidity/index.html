The read

₹1,05,090 cr traded across 743 classified stocks, and 45.8% of it was delivery. Breadth was healthy — 311 of 494 Nifty 500 names up, index +1.25%. Nothing says distribution at the market level.

Financial services is the liquidity magnet. It absorbed 26.5% of all turnover — more than the next two sectors combined — and converted 48.8% to delivery. That's the sector the money chose, and it held the stock.

Telecom shows the strongest conviction per rupee. Only 10 stocks, but 59.9% delivery, the highest of any sector. Bharti Airtel alone did ₹3,810 cr at 73.7% delivery while rising 3.77%. Heavy volume, mostly taken home.

Textiles is the trap. 2.1% of market turnover on 11.4% delivery, with 83% of its names up. Strong-looking tape, almost no stock changing hands. That is intraday churn, not accumulation.

Watch power and construction: both carried real turnover with breadth at 22% and 26%. Money moving through sectors where most names are falling is usually money leaving.

Capital markets is the standout on price. Up 4.53% on the day and 8.10% on the month, with breadth intact — unlike IT, whose one-month lead rests on 3 advances against 7 declines. BSE and MCX both appear in the turnover leaders below.

At industry level the picture sharpens. Telecom services converted 68.8% of its turnover to delivery and transport infrastructure 68.4%, both on near-perfect breadth — the two cleanest accumulation signals of the day. Banks took the single largest share of turnover at 11.4% with 58.8% delivery, but only 37% of bank stocks rose: money concentrating into the large private banks while the rest of the sector gave ground.

Three industries show the churn pattern plainly: agricultural food (19.4% delivery on 90% breadth), gas (20.7% on 100% breadth) and aerospace & defence (27.4% on 92% breadth). All three look like broad advances. In all three, almost nothing was kept.

Open any sector below to see which industries carry its turnover, then open an industry to see the stocks inside it. Financial services is the clearest case: banks take 46.8% of the sector's turnover and capital markets 28.1%, but they behave nothing alike — banks at 53.1% delivery on 35% breadth, capital markets at 40.7% on 85%. One is accumulation concentrated into a few large names while the rest of the industry falls; the other is a broad, fast move with lighter hands behind it.

The F&O / cash split is worth using. F&O names carry 70.3% of all turnover at 50.8% delivery; cash-only names carry 29.7% at 34.1%. Those are different worlds, so the tree colours delivery against whichever view you have selected rather than one market-wide number. Switch to cash only and the ranking changes completely — Capital Goods becomes the largest sector by turnover, ahead of financials. That is where the speculation sat: nine of the ten worst churn names trade in cash only, with no futures to hedge into.

The × avg badge is the one to watch. On the Today view, a stock shows it when the session's turnover ran at twice its own prior five-day average or more — unusual money, by the stock's own standard rather than an absolute rupee figure. Today's extremes were all churn: Trident at 76× its average turnover on 4.5% delivery, Share India 51× on 5.3%, KRBL 41× on 8.2%, Balrampur Chini 13× on 8.1%. Enormous volume arriving and leaving the same day. SBI's 3.0× at 33.5% delivery is the same pattern in a large cap.

Switch to This week or This month and every level re-aggregates over six or 21 sessions, so a single loud day stops dominating.

The Flow column is the one that answers your question directly. It is a sector's share of today's turnover minus its share across the whole month — so it says whether liquidity is expanding into a sector or draining out, regardless of what price did. IT reads −5.21, the largest outflow on the board: 10.4% of market turnover over the month, 8.4% over the week, 5.2% today. Money has been walking out of IT for a month, steadily. Yet IT still carries the best one-month price relative strength in the rotation chart below, at +11.97 against the Nifty 500. Price says leader; liquidity says exit. That gap is the whole reason to follow the money rather than the chart.

The opposite case is Oil & Gas at +3.62 — 5.4% of turnover over the month, 7.1% over the week, 9.0% today, with delivery steady near 46% throughout. Liquidity arriving and staying. Telecom at +1.99 is the same shape with delivery actually rising, 56.2% over the month to 59.9% today. And Textiles at +1.57 is the warning version: share rising fast while delivery collapses from 26.9% to 11.4%. Money arriving that has no intention of staying.

Sources: NSE full securities bhavcopy (03-Nov-2026) for turnover, delivery and breadth; official ind_close_all index files (03-Nov, 27-Oct, 01-Oct) for index levels and changes; Nifty Total Market, Microcap 250 and Nifty 500 constituent files for sector classification. All figures are official end-of-day, not intraday snapshots.
Universe is 743 EQ-series stocks carrying an NSE sector classification — 83.2% of total EQ turnover — all 743 of which also carry a four-tier industry label pulled from NSE's own quote API. Today is the 3 Nov session; This week aggregates six bhavcopies, 27 Oct to 3 Nov; This month aggregates 21 sessions, 1 Oct to 3 Nov. Flow is a group's share of the latest session's turnover minus its share across the 21-session month, in percentage points, and is computed within whichever F&O / cash view is selected. The × avg ratio compares the session's turnover with the same stock's mean turnover over the five preceding sessions. Industries below ₹3 cr turnover in the selected segment are omitted; each industry lists its eight largest stocks by turnover. F&O membership is from NSE's fo_mktlots file (225 underlyings, 218 of them in this universe); every other stock is cash-only. Delivery colouring and all signals are measured against the selected segment's own turnover-weighted delivery, shown above the table. ETFs and unclassified recent listings are excluded. Delivery value is turnover × delivery %. Sector breadth is the share of that sector's stocks closing up.
